## Design Context

### Users

Nigerian founders, entrepreneurs, and ambitious professionals studying Law, Economics, and Finance daily over 4 months. They open this app each morning (or late evening) for focused 1–3 hour study sessions. They are serious about self-improvement and want the tool to feel worthy of the material — a reflection of their ambition, not a generic learning app.

Creator: Michael Ojekunle — built this for himself, shared in public.

### Brand Personality

**Bold · Editorial · Aspirational**

LEF OS should feel like something a serious founder built for themselves and chose to share. It occupies a layered register: different readers will see it as a law library (quiet authority), a premium productivity tool (systematic, clean), a personal journal (intimate, reflective), or an editorial magazine (typographically rich, lingerable). The interface should support all four readings without forcing any one.

### Aesthetic Direction

- **Dark-first.** Users study in the morning and evening. Dark mode reduces eye strain over long sessions. Background tinted warm toward gold, never cold blue-black.
- **Gold as the anchor accent.** Warm amber — Nigerian brass/gold resonance, premium, aspirational. Law domain. Not overused.
- **Domain color system preserved:** Gold (Law), Sage (Economics), Slate Blue (Finance).
- **Anti-references:** Generic white-card SaaS dashboards (Notion clones, admin panels). Academic portals (Moodle, Blackboard). Crypto neon-on-dark UIs (gradients, glows). Gamified apps (Duolingo, streaks, confetti).
- **What it should feel like:** A premium study companion with editorial presence — think a well-designed independent magazine merged with a focused productivity OS.

### Font System

| Role               | Font           | Rationale                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------ | -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Display / Headings | **Montserrat** | Geometric sans, strong at 700–900, clean uppercase. Not in the overused reflex set. Editorial authority without serif stuffiness.                                                            |
| Body / UI text     | **Lexend**     | Designed by reading researchers to reduce visual stress and improve reading speed. Geometric and clean. Perfect for dense study content (law statutes, economic theory, financial concepts). |

CSS variables: `--font-display` (Montserrat), `--font-body` (Lexend).

Tailwind: `font-display` → Montserrat, `font-sans` → Lexend.

**Heading tracking guidelines:**

- h1 (display): `letter-spacing: -0.025em`, weight 800
- h2: `letter-spacing: -0.018em`, weight 700
- h3: `letter-spacing: -0.012em`, weight 600
- Labels/caps: `letter-spacing: 0.10em`, weight 600, all-caps — Montserrat excels here

### Color Principles

- Warm neutrals: backgrounds and surfaces tinted slightly toward gold hue (not cool grey)
- Dark mode text-secondary: ≥5:1 contrast ratio on surface backgrounds
- Domain accents used sparingly as signal, not decoration
- No gradient text, no neon, no glow borders used decoratively

### Design Principles

1. **Readability first** — every typography and spacing decision serves comprehension of dense content (law, economics, finance). Lexend + generous leading + 65–75ch line length.
2. **Premium without flash** — aspirational feel through spacing, type quality, and restraint. No gradients, no neon, no sparkle.
3. **Personal at scale** — this is one founder's journey made public. The interface is personal and intentional, not templated or corporate.
4. **Warm dark** — dark mode with warm neutral tints toward gold. The cold blue-black "developer dark mode" is a different product.
5. **Structured freedom** — strong typographic rhythm and grid, but with personality in the details (domain colors, gold accents, editorial heading sizes).
